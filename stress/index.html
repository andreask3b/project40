Stress

Periodise your life like training.

Five areas. One focus. A floor for the rest. Time and energy left for what you did not plan.

You cannot max family, job, health, food, and sport in the same block. Pick one.

The frame

Treat the week like a training season.

Around 40 the calendar is full. Family, job, health, food, and sport all want a peak. Stacking peaks is how sleep, patience, and sessions all get worse at once.

Periodise instead. Each block of about 4–8 weeks has one focus area. The other four run on a defined minimum. Leave slack so the unplanned does not wipe the plan.

  • One focus per block4–8 weeks. Raise one area. Hold the rest.
  • A minimum for eachThe floor you protect even in a hard month.
  • ~80% plannedAbout 20% of the week stays empty on purpose.
  • Deload when signals dropSleep, a resting-heart-rate trend, work piling up — no chasing numbers.

Minimums

The floor for each area.

Write yours down. These are examples of a maintenance dose — enough that the area does not collapse while another is the focus.

Sport

Two short strength sessions

A week. Keep them honest and brief. Easy movement on other days if you like — not another peak.

Family

One fixed family evening

Protected on the calendar. Not leftover minutes after a late session and a late inbox.

Food

Simple default meals

A repeatable default so fuel does not depend on a perfect shop or a new protocol.

Job

Focus time + a clear end

Protect a block of real work. End the day so sleep and sport still exist.

Health

Sleep window + checkups

A regular night window, and the checkups you already know you should keep. Not a dashboard of personal figures.

Deload

When readiness drops, shrink the week.

Training already uses deload weeks. Life load needs the same move. You do not need a dashboard of personal figures. Watch the trend: sleep getting shorter or more broken, a resting-heart-rate trend drifting the wrong way, work piling up, patience gone.

Then drop the focus intensity. Keep the minima. That is a deload — not a failed block.

  • Sleep window slippingProtect the night before you add another session or another late hour.
  • Resting-heart-rate trendDirection over days, not a single reading and not a target number.
  • Work piling upThe job is already the load. Stop stacking a hero week on top.

The weekly check

Which area is below its minimum?

Once a week, look at the five floors. If one is under, raise it. Do not try to catch up on all five. Catch-up weeks are how you max everything and recover from nothing.

Plan about 80% of the hours you think you have. Leave about 20% as buffer — illness, a late meeting, a child, a flat day. The buffer is part of the map, not leftover guilt.

Adjust, don’t repay

If sport missed its two sessions, do not triple next week. Restore the minimum. If family evening slipped, put the next one on the calendar. The block is long enough to be honest.

Sleep, work, movement

The load is not only in the gym.

The map only works if the basics still happen. Keep these small enough for a work night.

  • Sleep firstMost recovery happens overnight. A stable window beats a heroic weekend lie-in.
  • Work has a costLong, intense days spend the same account as a session. Cap stacked evenings.
  • Move, don’t only grindEasy walking often settles the week better than another interval when the calendar is already loud.
  • Signs the load is chronicSleep that stays poor, wired-or-tired most days, a short fuse, training that stops landing, getting ill often. Patterns, not a single bad day — and not a diagnosis.

When to see a doctor

Do not wait on these.

A life map is not care. Seek medical help promptly if something feels wrong — especially:

  • Chest pain or palpitationsSeek medical help promptly. Do not test this with another session.
  • Persistent sleep problemsNights that stay broken for weeks despite a calmer routine.
  • Mood low for weeksA flat or heavy mood that does not lift, or that makes daily life hard.
  • Other reasons to get checkedUnexplained shortness of breath, fainting, severe headaches, or symptoms that worsen instead of easing with rest.

If you are unsure, it is reasonable to ask a clinician. Early is better than waiting for a quieter week that may not come.
